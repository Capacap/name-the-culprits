# The story format

A library of The Loose Threads (code name berndle) is a folder of JSON files that the frontend loads one story at a time. Any generator may write it, and `node check.mjs <folder>` says whether it did so correctly. This page describes format 4.

The files are the whole story. The frontend lays them out and checks accusations, and generates nothing.

## The folder

```
index.json            the list of stories
<id>.json             one story
<id>.answer.json      its answer
```

A story's id is a string of lowercase letters and digits, unique in the library. It names the story for good: a story keeps its id and its text once published, and a library made at another model is a new library whose stories have new ids.

## index.json

```json
{
  "format": 4,
  "stories": [
    {"id": "7396ae98e6", "story": "7396ae98e6.json", "answer": "7396ae98e6.answer.json",
     "days": 3, "cast": 8, "date": null, "difficulty": "medium"}
  ]
}
```

`days` is the number of days the story tells and `cast` the number of guests. `difficulty` is `"easy"`, `"medium"` or `"hard"`, or null for a story not yet rated; the library screen does not show it. The screen calls a story of one or two days short and a longer one long. `date` (a `YYYY-MM-DD` string) is null until the game has stories by the day; several stories on one date are several entries.

## The story

```json
{
  "format": 4,
  "game": "berndle",
  "generator": "d26491b91718",
  "id": "7396ae98e6",
  "cast": [{"id": "robin", "name": "Robin", "pronouns": "they/them"}],
  "house": {"rooms": [], "doors": [], "tunnel": null},
  "slots": ["noon", "dusk", "midnight"],
  "rules": [{"title": "The culprits", "lines": ["There are no more than two culprits."],
             "more": ["One guest, or two working together. Never three."]}],
  "objective": ["Identify the culprit or culprits in the story."],
  "challenge": "Name the culprits, no more than two...",
  "largest_team": 2,
  "days": [],
  "check": "<sha256 hex>",
  "verdicts": {"<sha256 hex>": "<base64>"}
}
```

`generator` names the version of the generator that wrote the story, for whoever keeps the library: twelve hex digits, with `-dirty` where it was written from uncommitted changes, for a trial. A view shows nothing of it; the game sends it with a player's feedback and play signals, so that one wording of a story is told from another. `rules` are the rules the player may cite, in titled sections, the same in every story generated by one version of the generator. `more` holds, for each line in the same order, the narrator's longer telling of it, to show when the player asks; the player may cite it as they cite the line. `objective` and `challenge` are shown as written: the objective before the first day, the challenge after the last. `largest_team` is the most guests an accusation may name. A slot is referred to by its index in `slots`.

### The house

```json
{
  "rooms": [
    {"id": "hall", "name": "Hall", "kind": "room", "rules": []},
    {"id": "chapel", "name": "Chapel", "kind": "graveyard",
     "rules": ["Whoever is carried to the Chapel ends and begins every later day there, and no one else ever goes into it."]}
  ],
  "doors": [["chapel", "guesthouse"], ["fountain", "hall"]],
  "tunnel": null
}
```

A room's `kind` is `room`, `graveyard` (where bodies are carried) or `feature` (a room with a special rule, such as a bell tower or a dark passage). `rules` holds the rules that hold in this house only, shown under the map; a rule about two rooms is on both, so show each text once. A door is a pair of room ids, and the doors join the rooms (those within another aside) as a tree: one way between any two. `tunnel` is null or the pair of rooms a hidden tunnel joins, drawn apart from the doors.

A room with `within` is inside another room and has no doors of its own: the coffins of a tomb, `{"id": "coffins", "kind": "graveyard", "within": "tomb"}`. Accounts and the answer may place a guest there.

### Days and turns

```json
{
  "title": "The first day",
  "ends": 2,
  "turns": [
    {"speaker": "narrator", "kind": "narration", "continues": false,
     "lines": [{"text": "At dawn Terry was found lying still in the Parlour."}]},
    {"speaker": "robin", "kind": "examination", "continues": false,
     "lines": [{"text": "I'm so sorry. Terry is dead."}], "bodies": ["terry"]},
    {"speaker": "robin", "kind": "account", "continues": true,
     "lines": [{"text": "At noon I was in the Garden with Winnie.", "slots": [0, 0]}],
     "places": [["garden"], [], []]}
  ]
}
```

`ends` is the index in `slots` of the day's last slot. The first alarm ends a day with its slot, so a day the alarm ended at dusk has `ends` 1, and nothing in it tells a later slot: no line's `slots` and no account's `places` reach past `ends`. A day without an alarm runs to the last slot. A day's turns are in the order they are told. `speaker` is `narrator` or a cast id. `continues` is true where the turn goes on with the same speaker's speech from the turn before, so the two read as one speech. The kinds:

- `house`: the narrator introduces her house, the first turn of the first day and only there. Its first line names every room with a rule of its own (a room in `house.rooms` whose `rules` is not empty), so a view can tie those words to the rules; the next introduces the guests with the two rules a player needs before any of them speaks, that there are no more than two culprits and only a culprit can lie.
- `narration`: the narrator, who tells only truths. Every day's dawn opens with hers: the first turn of each day, the first after the house on the first. After the examinations she calls for the accounts in a line of her own ("Tell your tale. Every one of you.").
- `examination`: a guest confirms the death of `bodies`.
- `charge`: a guest says that the accounts of `against` cannot stand with their own. The account follows in the next turn, with `continues` true. `disputed` holds, for each guest in `against` in the same order, the slots at issue: the indices of the slots where the two accounts cannot both be true, so a view can tie the charge to the lines that tell those slots.
- `account`: a guest tells their day. Its first line may be a word that claims nothing ("Don't look at me."), an opener, which carries no `slots`.
- `defence`: a guest asks the guests whose accounts put them in the room of the day's first body whether they were there, naming the slots ("So you were in the Kitchen at midnight, Robin?"), from the accounts alone; each guest so asked answers with their slots there and may add what their own account says they found ("I was only there at midnight. I did raise the alarm."). The same question may also ask a guest whose account takes them into that room or next to it, whose account no other account names and whose own meets no living guest ("And you, Terry, in the Dining Room all day with nobody to see you?"), who answers from their own account ("I kept to myself all day."). The question stands as its own speech after the account of the last guest it asks, the answers turns of their own straight after it. It says nothing of who lied or who did it. At most one defence a day.
- `closing`: the narrator's last word on the day.

Each line is `{"text": ...}`. An account's lines also carry `slots`, the first and last slot the line tells, so a view can tie a line to a time. On a day with an alarm, every account but its raiser's ends with a line on the alarm, which carries the alarm's slot. A line that ends the day in the room a trip reached ("I stayed there the rest of the day.") and the trip's line before it both carry the slots of the two together. An opener, only ever an account's first line and never its only one, carries none.

An account also says where it puts its speaker, in ids, for the map. `places` holds, for each slot of the day up to `ends`, the rooms the account places the speaker in, in the order told, a room told twice listed twice. The way between two rooms told one after the other is the house's one path between them, through the doors of `house.doors`, so a view finds the rooms passed unnamed and the doors walked. An account never claims a step by the tunnel, which would leave that path. The account tells a trip where a slot names two different rooms.

### The check

`check` is the SHA-256, in lowercase hex, of the story's id, a newline, and the culprits' ids sorted and joined by commas:

```js
sha256(`${id}\n${[...team].sort().join(",")}`)
```

An accusation is right when its hash equals `check`. The hash keeps the answer out of a casual read of the file; there are few possible teams, so anyone who wants the answer can find it.

### Verdicts

`verdicts` holds, for every team an accusation may name except the culprits, the narrator's reason that team cannot be the one: lines that suppose the team guilty and show where the story then contradicts itself, using only what the player has read. A view shows them after a wrong accusation without fetching the answer.

Each entry is keyed by the team's hash, made as `check` is. Its value is sealed with the team's own ids so that only an accusation of that team opens it: the UTF-8 JSON list of lines, XORed byte by byte with consecutive SHA-256 digests of the id, a newline, the team's ids sorted and joined by commas, a newline, `verdict`, a newline and a counter from 0, then written in base64.

```js
const seed = `${id}\n${[...team].sort().join(",")}`;
// pad block k: sha256(`${seed}\nverdict\n${k}`), 32 bytes each
```

The team has no entry, so the keys tell the answer to whoever hashes every team, as `check` does.

## The answer

```json
{
  "format": 4,
  "id": "7396ae98e6",
  "team": ["ada", "ilya"],
  "told": {"days": [{"title": "The first day", "lines": ["At midnight Ada went from the Garden to the Library.", "Ada killed Terry in the Library."]}],
           "lies": [{"speaker": "ada", "day": 1, "said": ["I never left the Garden all day."], "truth": ["At noon and at dusk I was in the Garden, alone."]}]},
  "rivals": [{"team": ["robin", "terry"], "lines": ["..."]}]
}
```

`team` hashes to the story's `check`. `told` is the truth in plain words, for a reveal a player reads: each of `days` tells that day's crimes as sentences in the order of the slots (the culprits' trips, the victims' coming, the murders, a culprit lying down to play dead, an alarm, how each body was found and a false body's examination); `lies` holds each lying account, `day` counting from 1, with the lines `said` as the story tells them, leaving out an opener, which claims nothing, and the lines of the true account. It tells what happened.

`rivals` lists the wrong teams that came closest, at most two, each with its `team` and the `lines` of its verdict, the same lines as its entry in the story's `verdicts`. A story where no wrong team came close has no rivals.
